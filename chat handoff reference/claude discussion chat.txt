360 Labs' architecture has a structural flaw you should not copy. Their tag list says "Gemini Vision." A DWG is already exact vector data — every wall hatch is a HATCH entity with a computable boundary area, every sofa is an INSERT with a block name and insertion point. Running vision over it means re-deriving numbers from pixels that were already precise in the file. That's why vision-first tools plateau: on clean digital plans they get roughly 80–95% of the way there, and noticeably less on complex, MEP-heavy or scanned drawings, and every vendor accuracy claim of 95–99% is self-reported and unverified.

Your differentiator writes itself: the AI never touches a number. Geometry engine computes all quantities deterministically; the LLM only assigns meaning (what is this block, what material is this hatch). That is exactly the "no miscalculations, stays in the architect's circle" property you described — and it's architecturally enforced, not a promise.

The plan is in the file. The four things worth arguing about with your team member:

1. Geometry-first, not vision-first. Covered above. This one decision cascades into everything else — it's what makes click-a-line-see-the-entities provenance possible, and provenance is what makes an architect actually sign the document.

2. The Drawing Standard Profile is the real product. Every studio names layers and blocks differently, and that — not extraction — is where these systems die. Learn a studio's standard once, human-approve it once, reuse forever. Competitors re-infer from scratch on every file. 3. Measurement rules must be separate from measurement. Deducting a door opening from plaster area is a rule, not a measurement. Keeping geometry → rules → items → rates as four independently versioned layers is what lets you re-run without re-parsing, diff drawing revisions properly, and later serve IS 1200 / CPWD DSR tender work over the same geometry engine.

Three pivots from your GPT session

Adopted — crop-and-ask. Don't render the whole drawing; after rule-based classification you're left with a residual set of unknowns, so render tight crops and ask a closed-set question. Vision cost then scales with how messy a drawing is, not how big it is, and UNKNOWN becomes a useful answer that routes to a human.

Tightened — your doc said send Gemini the image plus structured CAD JSON. Right, but one step short: make the model's return schema label-only, no numeric field. Send dimensions as context if it helps disambiguate; structurally refuse to accept a number back. Otherwise a hallucinated dimension walks in the back door. Same for confidence — your doc correctly warned not to trust a self-reported 0.94, so compute it from how many of your four signals agreed. That number is auditable; the model's isn't.

Rejected — Autodesk RealDWG / APS for DWG. Right answer for a funded team, wrong one for you. DXF-only stands, and it's cleaner under the service model since you'd be receiving files yourself and can convert on your own Mac.

The profitable mechanics you said I missed — §3, four of them:

The one that matters most: sell the outcome, not the seat.
